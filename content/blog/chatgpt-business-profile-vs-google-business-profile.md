ChatGPT now lets businesses claim a profile, and the first question I get is whether it's the same thing as Google Business Profile. **It's the same idea, for a different place. You want both, and Google still comes first.**

## Side by side

| | Google Business Profile | ChatGPT business profile |
|---|---|---|
| Where it shows | Google Search and Maps | ChatGPT answers and results |
| Where you manage it | Google Business Profile | Business Hub in ChatGPT |
| How you verify | Google's own steps, which vary by business | A code to the business phone or email, or a DNS record, then an ID check |
| Can you add a new local listing? | Yes | Not yet. Local businesses can only claim an existing U.S. listing |
| Reviews | Customers leave them right on the profile | Not part of the profile |
| What you can edit | Hours, phone, services, photos, posts and more | Website, description, category, logo, cover photo, policies |
| What you can see | Calls, direction requests, searches | Mentions, website clicks, topics, last 28 days |
| Does claiming boost you? | A complete profile helps you rank | OpenAI says it doesn't guarantee more visibility |

## Why Google still comes first

For a Bergen County plumber, landscaper or dentist, Google is where most calls start today. A complete Google profile with steady reviews also helps you get named on ChatGPT, because ChatGPT leans on the same kind of public information when it decides who to recommend.

So if your Google profile is half done, finish that before anything else. [Local SEO basics](/blog/local-seo-basics-bergen-county/) walks through it.

## Why ChatGPT is still worth claiming

- **It's quick.** Claiming and verifying is a short job.
- **It puts the facts in your hands.** Your description and website come from you.
- **It's early.** Most of your competitors haven't done it.
- **It's yours.** If you leave it, someone else can try to claim it.

## Make them match

The most useful thing you can do is make your Google profile, your ChatGPT profile and your website say the same thing: the same business name, the same description of what you do, the same towns. When every source agrees, both Google and ChatGPT have an easier time trusting you.

ChatGPT now lets you claim your business. It's called Business Hub, and it does for ChatGPT roughly what Google Business Profile does for Google: you prove you run the business, then you manage what it shows.

**The claim takes a few minutes, and a couple of steps are hard to undo.** Here's the whole thing in order.

## Before you start

Have these ready and the claim goes much faster:

- **The right ChatGPT account.** Use one tied to the business, not a personal login you might lose. Whoever claims it becomes the admin, and the admin is the one who can add other people later.
- **The business phone or email on file.** ChatGPT sends a code to the contact details it already has for you. If that's an old number, you'll want another way to verify.
- **Access to your website's domain settings,** or the person who manages them. This is the backup if the phone or email doesn't work.
- **A government-issued ID.** You'll need it for the identity check after the claim is approved. OpenAI may also ask for business registration details and proof you're authorized to represent the business.

One thing to check now: your business name, address and any registration or tax number have to match your official records. If your Google listing says "Smith Plumbing & Heating" and your state filing says "Smith Plumbing LLC", know which one you're working from.

## Step 1: Find your listing

Sign in to ChatGPT and open **Business Hub** from the sidebar. Search for your business by name.

If you see it, select the listing that matches and confirm the ChatGPT account you'll use to manage it.

If you don't see it, or the listing says **Contact support**, skip to [what to do if you can't find your business](/blog/cant-find-business-chatgpt-business-hub/). Right now local stores and restaurants can only claim a listing that already exists, and only in the U.S.

## Step 2: Verify the claim

ChatGPT only shows the verification methods that work for your business. You'll see some of these:

- **Business phone.** Choose a text or an automated call, then enter the code.
- **Business email.** A code goes to the email address associated with your business.
- **Website domain.** ChatGPT gives you a TXT record to add to your domain's DNS settings. Copy the type, the name or host, and the value exactly. DNS changes can take a while to show up, so come back and select **Verify domain** once it's in.
- **Shopify.** If you sell through Shopify, you can connect the store instead.

Phone or email is usually the quickest. The DNS route is the one people trip on, mostly by pasting the host field wrong. If someone else manages your domain, send them the exact record and ask them to add it.

## Step 3: Wait for approval

OpenAI reviews every claim and emails you a decision, or asks for more information. You can cancel while it's under review if you picked the wrong listing.

## Step 4: Pass the ID check

Once the claim is approved, you can open your business in Business Hub. But you can't publish changes or see any numbers until you finish identity verification with **Persona**, the service OpenAI uses.

Select **Verify with Persona** and follow the steps. The verified badge, a blue check, shows up only after Persona approves you. Claiming alone doesn't give you the check.

If it fails, the email tells you why. The usual cause is a mismatch between what you entered and your official records.

## Step 5: Fill out your profile

After both approvals, open your profile and fill in:

- Your website
- A plain description of what you do and the towns you serve
- Your category
- Your logo and a cover photo
- Delivery and return policies, if they apply to you

Write the description the same way your Google profile and website describe you. ChatGPT reads all three, and matching details make you easier to trust. OpenAI reviews changes before they go live, so give it time.

## Step 6: Give your marketing person access the right way

If someone helps with your marketing, add them from Business Hub as a teammate or grant their agency access. Each person uses their own ChatGPT account. Don't forward your claim invitation email to them. OpenAI specifically warns against that.

## Mistakes that are hard to undo

- **Claiming on the wrong account.** Access hangs off the admin account. Pick it on purpose.
- **Publishing a profile you're not happy with.** OpenAI's support team can't delete, unpublish or hide a published profile. Only drafts can be deleted. And if you create a brand new profile through Persona, approval publishes it automatically, with no separate publish step.
- **Assuming one claim covers every location.** Each location and each brand is claimed separately.

## What you'll see once you're in

Business Hub shows the last 28 days of **mentions** (for a local business, each time ChatGPT shows a result for your location), **website clicks**, and **discovery topics**, which are themes, not the actual questions people typed. A blank number doesn't always mean zero. It can mean there isn't enough activity yet to report.

Claiming won't move you up in ChatGPT's answers on its own. OpenAI says that directly. What it does is make sure the facts are yours.
